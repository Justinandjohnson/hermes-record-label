# Stats

There are ten stats in two tiers.

**Core attributes** are abilities you carry everywhere. They act as **multipliers**: they set how many levels each point you spend on a built stat turns into.

**Built stats** are what you've accumulated. They come from spending points, filtered through your attributes.

Three stats are **foundation stats**: Body, Discipline and Bonds. They have the strongest evidence for long-term health, wealth and lifespan. If one of them is low, always name it, whatever importance the Player gave it.

Levels are absolute rather than graded against people the same age. Use `age-norms.md` to say where the Player sits relative to their peers. To score, pick the anchor that the evidence fits best. If the Player lands between two anchors, use the even number between them.

# Core attributes

## Body *(foundation)*
Cardio fitness, strength, mobility, sleep, energy.
**Why it matters:** compared with low cardiorespiratory fitness, elite fitness went with 80% lower all-cause mortality, with no upper limit observed (Mandsager 2018).
- **1**: Daily life is physically hard, or a health problem is going unmanaged.
- **3**: Mostly sedentary, low energy, and getting winded easily.
- **5**: Moves regularly, has no major limitations, and sleeps decently.
- **7**: Trains 3 or more times a week and is clearly stronger and fitter than most people their age.
- **9**: Athlete-level conditioning.
- **10**: Competes at an elite level.

**Over time:** fast decay. Weeks off show up within months.
**Probes:** What did movement look like last week? How's your sleep? Any injuries or conditions? Could you run a mile right now, and how fast? If they know their VO2max, compare it to `age-norms.md`.

## Mind
Learning speed, knowledge, reasoning, curiosity.
**Why it matters:** cognitive ability predicts mortality, divorce and occupational attainment about as strongly as personality and family socioeconomic status do (Roberts 2007).
- **1**: No learning since school ended.
- **3**: Learns only when forced to.
- **5**: Reads or learns regularly and can explain several topics well.
- **7**: A fast, deep self-directed learner who connects ideas across fields.
- **9**: Others come to them to understand hard things.
- **10**: Produces original thinking that others build on.

**Over time:** slow decay. Learning compounds, because each thing you know makes the next one easier.
**Probes:** What have you learned in the last year that you didn't have to? How fast? What topic could you teach for an hour? What's something you were sure about and turned out wrong?

## Discipline *(foundation)*
Self-control, follow-through, consistency, finishing.
**Why it matters:**
- Childhood self-control predicted adult health, wealth, substance problems and crime in a dose-dependent gradient, even between siblings raised in the same home (Dunedin cohort, Moffitt 2011).
- Conscientiousness predicts life outcomes as strongly as IQ and family socioeconomic status (Roberts 2007).
- **1**: Can't follow through on anything without outside pressure.
- **3**: Works in bursts of intensity, then drops off. Deadlines drive everything.
- **5**: Keeps most commitments and has a few lasting habits.
- **7**: Runs reliable systems and does hard things on schedule without needing hype.
- **9**: Has stayed consistent for years across several areas of life.
- **10**: Iron consistency, regardless of mood.

**Over time:** a trait, but trainable. It's built by keeping small promises.
**Ledger:** no column. Discipline shows up as how consistent the other columns are, not as hours of its own.
**Probes:** What's your longest unbroken streak with any habit? What happens to a project after the excitement fades? Of the things you've started in the last two years, how many did you finish?

## Charisma
Social *skill*, the ability itself: reading people, conversation, warmth, humor, persuasion, conflict, flirting.
**Why it matters:** the labor market increasingly rewards social skill, and it complements automation and cognitive skill rather than competing with them (Deming 2017). It is also the engine that turns time into Bonds.
- **1**: Avoids interaction, and conversations often go wrong.
- **3**: Fine in transactional talk, but struggles with small talk, reading people, or conflict.
- **5**: Comfortable one-on-one and in groups. Can make friends and get dates with effort.
- **7**: Builds rapport easily, handles conflict well, and people seek them out.
- **9**: Magnetic. Leads, persuades, and the room responds.
- **10**: An elite communicator or leader.

**Over time:** medium decay, and it rusts without reps. It's very trainable with deliberate practice.
**Ledger:** deliberate social practice and time with new people go here. Time with people they already have goes to Bonds.
**Probes:** How do conversations with strangers go? When did you last handle a conflict well? How many dates in the last year? What feedback do people give you about how you come across?

## Spirit
Purpose, mental-health baseline, self-knowledge, resilience.
**Why it matters:** this stat decides whether the other levels actually feel like a good life. It's also where redemption stories and resilience show up.
- **1**: In crisis. If this is the read, follow ground rule 6.
- **3**: Mostly drifting, often anxious or numb.
- **5**: Generally okay, with some sense of direction.
- **7**: Clear on their values, grounded, and recovers from setbacks well.
- **9**: Deeply at peace, and steady for others too.
- **10**: Unshakeable equanimity.

**Over time:** varies. Practices like therapy, faith or reflection compound; neglect erodes it quietly.
**Probes:** How do you feel most mornings? What do you care about most? What do you do when life knocks you down? When did you last feel fully yourself?

# Built stats

## Craft
The depth of the Player's marketable or professional skill.
**Why it matters:** this is how points turn into income and standing.
- **1**: No developed skill.
- **3**: A beginner or junior who needs supervision.
- **5**: A solidly competent professional.
- **7**: The go-to person on their team or in their scene.
- **9**: Recognized across their field.
- **10**: Top of their field.

**Over time:** compounds, with medium decay if left unused for years.
**Probes:** What do people pay you for or come to you for? How many years have you spent on it, and how many of those were deliberate practice? What's the hardest thing you've shipped? Is there external proof, like income, clients, users or recognition?

## Wealth
Money, assets, and financial runway. Measure it in multiples of **annual spending**.
**Why it matters:** it's security and freedom, and it compounds, so points spent early are worth the most.
- **1**: Debt is crushing and growing.
- **3**: Living paycheck to paycheck, with little or no savings.
- **5**: Has a 3–6 month emergency fund and is saving or investing regularly.
- **7**: Net worth is at least 3× annual spending.
- **9**: Net worth is at least 12× annual spending.
- **10**: Net worth is at least 25× annual spending, which means financially independent.

**Over time:** compounds.
**Probes:** About how much do you spend a year? What's your net worth, roughly (assets minus debts)? Any high-interest debt? What's your income trend? Ranges are fine if exact numbers feel too personal.

## Bonds *(foundation)*
The relationships themselves, as an inventory: family, partner, friends, community.
**Why it matters:**
- Across 148 studies and about 309,000 people, stronger social relationships went with 50% higher odds of survival, an effect comparable to quitting smoking (Holt-Lunstad 2010).
- Relationship quality predicted health and happiness better than wealth, fame, IQ or genes (Harvard Study of Adult Development).
- **1**: Isolated, with no one to call.
- **3**: Has a few relationships, but they're thin, strained, or one-sided.
- **5**: Has a handful of solid people and stable family ties.
- **7**: Has a strong inner circle and is someone others lean on.
- **9**: Anchors a wide, deep network or community.
- **10**: A pillar who holds many lives together.

**Over time:** compounds, with medium decay. Relationships fade without contact.
**Probes:** Who would show up at 2am? How many close friends have you talked to this month? How are things with your family and partner? Are you part of any group or community?

## Creation
Finished things in the world that exist without the Player standing next to them: music, apps, businesses, art, writing.
**Why it matters:** it's leverage. Work that keeps paying, being used, or speaking for you while you sleep.
- **1**: Nothing made.
- **3**: Has made things, but mostly unfinished or private.
- **5**: Has a finished, public body of work.
- **7**: Others use, buy, or regularly engage with the work.
- **9**: The work has a significant audience or impact.
- **10**: The work defines or shifts its space.

**Over time:** no decay. Finished work stays finished, and some of it keeps compounding.
**Probes:** What have you finished and released? What's sitting at 80% done? Who uses or listens to it, and how many people?

## Presence (Look & Style)
The controllable layer of appearance: grooming, clothing fit, style coherence, posture, and how the Player comes across when they walk into a room.
**Why it matters:**
- Plain-looking workers earn 5–10% less than average-looking ones; below-average-looking men earned 13% less (Hamermesh).
- It also affects first impressions in dating and in business.
- Most of the gap is in the controllable layer, which makes this the cheapest stat to raise.
- **1**: No attention paid to grooming or clothing.
- **3**: Clean and functional, but clothes don't fit well and there's no point of view.
- **5**: Put-together, with clothes that fit and solid grooming.
- **7**: Has a recognizable style that suits them, and carries themselves with confidence.
- **9**: People ask where they got things or copy their look.
- **10**: Their style is a signature people remember.

**Over time:** slow decay, and fast gains.
**Probes:** Photos (full body and face, in natural light). What do you wear most days? When did you last update your wardrobe or haircut? How do you want people to read you?

## Starting roll (not scored)
Record genetics and health at birth, family resources, place and era, and early advantages or obstacles. Use these to explain efficiency, not to judge it.

## Custom stats
If the Player adds a stat, work with them to define it and write anchors for 1, 3, 5, 7, 9 and 10 before scoring it. Ten stats is the maximum, so a custom stat replaces one of these.
